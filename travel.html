Around the world

Five countries so far, and always angling for the next one

The map so far

I love traveling — the food, the disorientation of a city I’ve never seen, the small project of working out how a new place runs. So far the list is the US, Canada, Mexico, China, and Japan, and I’m always angling for the next stamp.

A lot of my US and Canada travel is on skis — Colorado, Utah, and Vermont in the States, and Whistler up in British Columbia — and that side lives over on the skiing page. Plenty of it isn’t, though: the monuments in DC, a few days in LA, a run of college visits, and Fallingwater, which is barely an hour from home.

China is the one I keep going back to. There's just so much about it — family, food, and places older than anything I grew up around — the Great Wall, a train north to Harbin, mornings watching the sun come up over Huangshan. It never quite loses that first-visit feeling.

Japan and Mexico rounded things out, and both are the kind of trip that has you half-planning the next one before you’ve even flown home. Different food, a different pace, the same pull to keep going somewhere I haven’t been yet.

Though Mexico feels more of a nature-ish place, Japan and its technology make it somewhere I could genuinely picture living down the line. Call me crazy, but my dream, as of right now, is going to university with my girlfriend, then later on staying in Manhattan while she chases a career on Wall Street and I do research, and eventually Japan.

And if you’ve really read everything and found your way to this page, you deserve some kind of easter egg.

So here’s one: my girlfriend is one of the best people I met in my friend group at Ross. She’s in some of the pictures, too. When I say Ross was the best six weeks of my life, believe me.
