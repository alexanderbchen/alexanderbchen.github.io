Bio.Hi! I'm Alex Chen, a high school senior in Pittsburgh.
I aspire to give mathematics a different perspective. Most people meet math as rules to memorize; I'd rather show them the version I fell for. I mainly do olympiad mathematics, run a school-hosted math team and competition that I founded, and do some research on things I find interesting. Alongside my most recent project on studying localization in random Schrödinger operators through the PReMa program with Texas A&M, I’m also working through the University of Pittsburgh’s mathematics sequence with their Accelerated High School Program, currently learning about Partial Differential Equations. When I’m not on a problem set I’m writing textbooks or animating some interesting ideas that popped into my mind. Aside from math, some things I truly enjoy include (but are not limited to) piano, swimming, and skiing. I find classical music oddly as calming as it is exciting, and oftentimes I find my mood contingent on the piece I'm currently practicing or even listening to. I also find it strangely enjoyable pushing my physical body to its limit while staring at a single black line at the bottom of the pool for hours on end. And finally, nothing quite beats embracing nature with soft, powdery snow beneath your feet on a high mountaintop watching the beautiful sunrise during Christmas. I'm interested in a variety of things, and I'd love to hear from you, so feel free to contact me here!

Current Research and Ideas

I study Anderson localization and discrete random Schrödinger operators: roughly, why randomness in a medium traps waves that would otherwise spread. On the integer line the operator reads

\[ (H\psi)(n) = \psi(n+1) + \psi(n-1) + V_\omega(n)\,\psi(n), \qquad V_\omega(n)\ \text{i.i.d.} \]

and the object I care about, the eigenfunction correlator, decays exponentially in the disordered regime:

\[ \mathbb{E}\!\left[\, \sup_{t}\, \big|\langle \delta_m,\, e^{-itH}\delta_n\rangle\big| \,\right] \;\le\; C\, e^{-|m-n|/\xi}. \]

That inequality is the precise sense in which the waves stay put, and getting honest constants out of it is most of the work. The full write-up, with the numerical results, is on the works page.

What’s next is the part I find most fun to think about: turning a decay rate I can only estimate numerically into something I can prove, and chasing the same localization questions into higher dimensions and singular disorder, where the clean theorems run out.
